# AI Lead – Role Statement

**Student ID:** 6631503004  
**Name:** Kanyanat Sawatdiwong  
**Role:** AI Lead

As the AI Lead of Singto, I was responsible for the scheduling recommendation logic that helps organizers identify suitable dates based on participant availability. My work focused on designing, implementing, and reviewing the Best Date functionality to ensure that its recommendations followed consistent and explainable rules.

The implemented solution uses deterministic, rule-based scheduling logic rather than a trained machine-learning model or generative AI model. It considers accepted participants, calculates availability counts for individual dates and consecutive date ranges, and identifies all candidate options with the highest availability. The system also supports a holiday filter based on weekends and configured fixed-date Thai public holidays.

I contributed to documenting the algorithm's processing workflow, recommendation rules, limitations, and proposed evaluation methodology. Evaluation focuses on availability calculation correctness, recommendation rule conformance, test case pass rate, and processing time.

**Evidence:**
- Shared scheduling logic: `src/lib/bestDate.js`
- Organizer recommendation implementation: `src/pages/Overview/Overview.jsx`
- Calendar and holiday rules: `src/lib/calendar.js`
- Availability API: `api/events/[id]/availability.js`
- Algorithm documentation: `.docs/final/ai-logic.md`

**AI Disclosure:** AI tools may have assisted with code explanation, debugging, and documentation. The scheduling recommendations themselves are generated by deterministic application logic, not by an AI model.

## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned**

Using AI changed how we worked more than I expected. The best part was speed. We could go from an idea to a working page really fast. It also made us think about rules earlier. Because we had to write AGENTS.md for the AI tools, we decided our PDPA and AI rules before coding, not at the end. Honestly, we probably would have skipped that otherwise.

The worst part was that it became too easy to accept code we didn't fully understand. When the event ID kept going missing after LIFF loaded, we fixed the same bug again and again in small commits, partly because we didn't really know how that part worked. AI also blurred our roles. Since everyone could do a bit of everything with AI, we ended up working as "fullstack, design, docs" instead of the roles we planned. For my part, I had to keep checking that the Best Date logic really did what we said it does, not just trust it because it ran.

**2. Where I stand**

What matters more now is knowing what to build, why, and whether the result is actually right. That was also most of what my AI Lead role was about.
